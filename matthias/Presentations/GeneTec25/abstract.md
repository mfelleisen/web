# Socially Responsible Software Development

## Matthias Felleisen
 
Software is a message from one developer to other developers across
time. As such, the creation of software incurs a social debt to all
those developers who will have to look at this software in the
future---be that an older version of the original person or someone
who isn't even born yet.

Understood this way, software development is far more than a technical
discipline. It must involve training of reading carefully, writing
properly, and joint work. Every single developer must learn to
approach the activity in an egoless manner; every team must routinely
subject every piece of code to rigorous in-person inspections; and
every piece of code must come with---and must express---a focused
purpose.

This talk will present my way of teaching students of computer science
in this manner, with the hope that some of it may help the working
programmer, too. 


## BIO

Mathias Felleisen is Trustee Professor of Computer Science at
Northeastern University. He is also a Fellow of the ACM, received the
organization's Karl Karlstrom Award for his work on curriculum
development, and was honored with the ACM SIGPLAN Lifetime Award for
his research on programming languages.
